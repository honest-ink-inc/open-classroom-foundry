## Letter 1 — to the two accepted members

**Subject: Your first session — and an honest change to the pilot's timing**

> Dear [name],
>
> Thank you again for saying yes. Here is everything that has happened since, and the one thing I need from you.
>
> **The pilot has moved, and later is better.** I had aimed at early September; that was the earliest honest date rather than a comfortable one, and I am glad to have been told so. The nonprofit's incorporation in Maryland has to complete before some of what surrounds the pilot can, and rather than squeeze the work into the gap I have moved the pilot back by several weeks. The six-week shape is unchanged. Your part stays inside 30–45 minute sessions: the first week is "think-aloud" sessions with the printable studio — you make real classroom materials with invented details while narrating, and I take notes and say as little as possible. The session evaluates the software, never you; the places you get stuck are the most valuable thing you can give.
>
> **What I need: the shape of your availability, not specific dates yet.** Which days and times of a typical week suit you, roughly how far ahead you need to plan, and whether you prefer your own classroom, another site, or a video call with the build installed on your machine beforehand. I will come back with real dates as soon as the incorporation clears, and I will confirm them with you before anything is fixed.
>
> **Recognition and compensation:** you are credited by name and seat in release notes — or not at all, entirely your preference, changeable at any time. Each completed session earns an honorarium of $50 ($85 for the specialist seats), capped at $300 a quarter. One honest wrinkle: payment waits until the new nonprofit can lawfully write a check (incorporation is in progress), so a written ledger of your sessions accrues from the first one and pays retroactively the moment it can. And a sentence I will repeat until it is boring: compensation attaches to sessions completed, never to conclusions reached — "this module fails" pays the same as "this ships."
>
> **A small piece of housekeeping:** the project now has a legal home — Honest Ink, Inc., incorporating in Maryland — which is why this letter comes from contact@honest-ink.org. Nothing about the work changes: the software remains free and open source (GPL), the materials in every session remain synthetic or staged, never student work, and you may withdraw at any time.
>
> With respect and anticipation,
> [typist's name]
> Director, Honest Ink, Inc. · contact@honest-ink.org

If the pilot's second week suits you, wonderful; if you need considerably longer, the module waits and that is the system working as designed. And if this isn't the season for you, I would be grateful for an introduction to a colleague you trust — the rule waits for the expertise, not for any one calendar.
